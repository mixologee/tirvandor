# Blood & Coin: Player's Guide

## Campaign Overview

### What is Blood & Coin?
Blood & Coin is a mercenary-focused campaign set in the Border region of Thaldros, a contested frontier zone between the Kingdom's central authority and independent territories. You play professional fighters-for-hire working for the Iron Guild, taking contracts from anyone with gold to spend. This is a morally gray campaign where money talks, loyalty is negotiable, and survival depends on your skills and reputation.

**Core Themes** ::
- Professional mercenary work
- Moral ambiguity and gray choices
- Reputation and consequences
- Gold and contracts over idealism
- Tactical combat and strategic thinking
- Inter-party dynamics and trust

**Campaign Tone** ::
- Gritty and pragmatic
- Dark humor and gallows wit
- Action-focused with intrigue
- Morally complex situations
- High combat, medium role play, medium exploration

## Who You Are

### The Iron Guild
You are members of (or affiliated with) the Iron Guild, the largest and most respected mercenary organization in the Border. The Guild operates under strict professional standards:

**The Guild Code:** ::
1. Honor Contracts, A contract, once signed, is sacred
2. No Betrayal, Never betray your squad or employer (during the contract)
3. Fair Payment – Good work deserves good pay
4. Professional Conduct: Mercenaries, not bandits
5. Reputation Matters, Your word is your bond

**What This Means:** ::
- You're professionals, not murder-hobos
- You can refuse any contract before signing
- Once you sign, you fulfill it (or face consequences)
- You can work for anyone: Thaldros, Aethoria, merchants, nobles, criminals
- Your reputation affects future contracts and pay rates

## Character Creation Guide

### Step 1: Pick Your Mercenary Type
What kind of mercenary are you?

**The Bruiser** ::
Front-line fighter who takes hits and deals damage ::
Classes: Barbarian, Fighter, Paladin ::
Role: Tank, damage dealer ::
Personality: Tough, reliable, intimidating ::

**The Professional** ::
Trained soldier with tactical expertise ::
Classes: Fighter, Ranger, Monk ::
Role: Versatile combatant, leader ::
Personality: Disciplined, strategic, respected ::

**The Specialist** ::
Expert in a specific combat role ::
Classes: Rogue, Ranger, Monk ::
Role: Scout, assassin, infiltrator ::
Personality: Skilled, precise, valuable ::

**The Support** ::
Keeps the team alive and effective ::
Classes: Cleric, Bard, Druid ::
Role: Healer, buffer, utility ::
Personality: Pragmatic, essential, respected ::

**The Mystic** ::
Magic user for hire ::
Classes: Wizard, Sorcerer, Warlock ::
Role: Artillery, problem-solver, trump card ::
Personality: Educated, powerful, expensive ::

**The Face** ::
Negotiator and information gatherer ::
Classes: Bard, Rogue, Warlock ::
Role: Social skills, contracts, intel ::
Personality: Charming, clever, connected ::

### Step 2: Choose Race
All races work, but some fit the mercenary life better:

**Highly Recommended:** ::
Human: Versatile, common, no stigma. Most mercenaries are human. ::
Half-Orc: Respected for strength. "Hired muscle" stereotype but well-paid. ::
Dwarf: Reliable, tough. Good reputation in the Guild. ::
Half-Elf – Adaptable, good with people. Excellent negotiators. ::

### Step 6: Squad Bonds
You're part of a mercenary squad. How do you know each other?
Choose or roll 1d8:
1. Veterans – Served together in previous contracts
2. Guild Training, Met during Iron Guild recruitment/training
3. Saved Each Other: One saved another's life in combat
4. Recommended – A mutual contact brought you together
5. Former Enemies: Fought on opposite sides once, now allies
6. Family/Friends – Preexisting relationship outside mercenary work
7. Strangers: Just assigned to same squad by the Guild
8. Shared Trauma: Survived something terrible together
Important: You trust your squad (at least professionally). Mercenaries who betray their own don't last long.

## Iron Guild Reputation System

### How It Works
Starting Reputation: 0 (Unproven)

You earn Reputation Points by: ::
- Completing contracts successfully (+2 to +5)
- Exceeding contract expectations (+1 to +3 bonus)
- Refusing immoral contracts (+1, but no pay)
- Exceptional combat/skill performance (+1)
- Saving squad members (+1)

You lose Reputation Points by: ::
- Failing contracts (-3 to -10)
- Betraying employers (-10, possible expulsion)
- Betraying squad (-20, definite expulsion)
- Breaking Guild Code (-5 to -15)
- Cowardice or incompetence (-1 to -5)

\column

Reputation Tiers: ::

| Tier | Rep Points | Title | Effects |
|------|------------|-------|---------|
| -20 or below | -20+ | **Outcast** | Expelled from Guild, hunted |
| -10 to -19 | -10 to -19 | **Disgraced** | No contracts, watched |
| -1 to -9 | -1 to -9 | **Suspect** | Limited contracts, low pay |
| 0 to 9 | 0 to 9 | **Unproven** | Basic contracts only |
| 10 to 19 | 10 to 19 | **Reliable** | Standard contracts, fair pay |
| 20 to 29 | 20 to 29 | **Trusted** | Good contracts, +10% pay |
| 30 to 39 | 30 to 39 | **Veteran** | Premium contracts, +20% pay |
| 40 to 49 | 40 to 49 | **Elite** | Choice contracts, +30% pay, Guild backing |
| 50+ | 50+ | **Legend** | Any contract, +50% pay, full Guild resources |

Reputation affects: ::
- Contract availability
- Pay rates
- NPC reactions
- Guild support and resources
- Plot opportunities

## Moral Choices & Consequences

### The Mercenary's Dilemma
You will face hard choices:
Example Scenarios:

**The Slaver Contract** ::
- Well-paying client wants you to recapture escaped slaves
- Legal in Thaldros, profitable, but morally wrong
- Refuse = lost money + possible reputation hit
- Accept = good pay + moral stain

**The Civilian Question** ::
- Contract to raid an enemy supply depot
- Depot is in a civilian town
- Collateral damage likely
- How far do you go?

**The Better Offer** ::
- Mid-contract, enemy offers double to switch sides
- No one would know...
- But your reputation...

**The Squad vs. Contract** ::
- Contract demands you leave wounded squad mate
- Saving them means failing contract
- What matters more?

*There Are No Perfect Answers
Every choice has consequences
Your character's values will be tested
The DM won't judge your choices
But the world will react to them*

## Campaign Structure

### What to Expect
Typical Session:
1. Downtime. Recover, train, shop, role play (30 min)
2. Contract Briefing – Get mission details, negotiate pay (15 min)
3. Preparation – Plan, gather intel, prepare (15 min)
4. Mission, Execute the contract (2 hours)
5. Aftermath: Get paid, reputation changes, consequences (30 min)

Campaign Progression ::
- Levels 1-4: Local contracts, building reputation
- Levels 5-8: Regional contracts, factional involvement
- Levels 9-12: Major contracts, caught in war, final arc

Combat Frequency: HIGH
- Expect combat every session
- Tactical challenges, not just brawls
- Resource management important

Role play Opportunities: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Squad dynamics and banter
- Moral dilemmas and choices
- NPC employer interactions
- Downtime activities

Exploration: MEDIUM
- Traveling between contracts
- Infiltration missions
- Intel gathering

## Tips for Players

### How to Succeed in Blood & Coin

#### IN COMBAT
- Work as a team: You're professionals
- Use tactics: Positioning matters
- Conserve resources: Multiple fights per day
- Protect your healer: They keep you alive
- Retreat when needed: Dead mercs earn nothing

#### Out of Combat 
- Gather intel before missions
- Plan your approach
- Negotiate good contracts
- Build your reputation
- Make useful contacts

#### Role Play
- Have a personality beyond "I hit things"
- Develop squad dynamics and banter
- Make hard choices in character
- Build relationships with recurring NPCs
- Have personal goals beyond money

#### DON'T 
- Murder-hobo: You're professionals
- Betray the squad: Death sentence
- Ignore contracts: Reputation matters
- Be a lone wolf: This is team-based
- Expect easy answers: Moral complexity is the point

## Setting Information

### The Border Region
**Where You Operate** ::
The Border is a 50-mile-wide strip of frontier land where the Thaldros Crown's authority fades and independent settlements govern themselves. Neither side fully controls it, making it a haven for:

- Mercenaries (like you)
- Smugglers and traders
- Refugees from both Kingdom and frontier
- Bandits and criminals
- Independent settlements

**The Conflict** ::
The Thaldros Kingdom wants to bring the frontier under royal control, while the frontier territories fight to remain independent. Neither can afford full-scale war, so they use mercenaries, economic pressure, and political maneuvering. The Border is where this cold war plays out.

**The International Angle** ::
The frontier territories watch this conflict carefully, weighing their options. Some leaders dream of true independence, others seek better terms with the Crown. Rumors say Aethoria across the Sundering Sea watches with interest, perhaps seeing opportunity in Thaldros divided against itself.

**Major Border Towns** ::
- Crossroads (Population: ~2,400)
- Largest Border settlement
- Iron Guild headquarters

**Neutral ground for all factions** ::
- Your home base
- Grimwatch (Population: ~800)
- Fortified trading post
- Kingdom loyalist settlement

**Good for royal military contracts** ::
- Freehold (Population: ~1,200)
- Independent village
- Sympathetic to democratic ideals (Aethoria influence)
- Lots of frontier refugee work

### The Iron Guild
**Your Employer** ::
Officially founded in 1197 CR by veteran soldiers tired of war, the Iron Guild evolved from loose mercenary alliances that began forming around 1100 CR during the escalating conflicts between Thaldros and Aethoria. Over nearly a century, these independent companies gradually organized into professional guilds, culminating in the formal establishment of the Iron Guild as the premier mercenary organization in Tirvandor.

**Guild Structure** ::
- - Guildmaster: Oversees all operations
- Contract Brokers: Match mercenaries with clients
- Squad Leaders: Experienced mercs who lead teams
- Members: You (regular mercenaries)
- Prospects: Trainees earning membership

**Guild Services** ::
- Contract mediation and enforcement
- Legal protection
- Equipment at cost
- Healing and recovery
- Training and advancement
- Safe houses and contacts

## Frequently Asked Questions
:
**Q: Can I be a good person in this campaign?** ::
A: Yes! Many mercenaries have moral codes. You choose which contracts to take.

**Q: Will we be forced to do evil things?** ::
A: No. You can refuse any contract. But refusing loses potential income and some contracts may test your morals.

**Q: Can we switch sides during a contract?** ::
A: Technically yes, but expect severe reputation consequences. The Guild Code forbids it.

**Q: What if my character wouldn't take a contract?** ::
A: Then don't! But have an in-character reason and accept the consequences.

**Q: Can we work for both Kingdom forces and frontier territories?** ::
A: Absolutely. The Guild is neutral. Your only loyalty is to gold and the current contract.

**Q: How lethal is this campaign?** ::
A: Moderately lethal. Combat is dangerous but healers are available. Smart play keeps you alive.

**Q: Can I play a pacifist?** ::
A: Very difficult in a mercenary campaign. Consider a support role (healer, face) instead.

**Q: What if we want to eventually pick a side?** ::
A: That's an option! The campaign can evolve based on your choices.
